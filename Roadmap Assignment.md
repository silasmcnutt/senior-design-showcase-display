# Assignment: Project Roadmap & Design Materials

## Overview

Your charter defined what your team will deliver and why. This assignment moves you to how: a two-semester roadmap that shows how the work will unfold, and design materials that show what the product will actually be.

You will save the following to your project wiki:

1. Project Roadmap — A high-level plan covering both semesters of the capstone.
2. Design Materials — Enough design work that someone outside your team could understand the product you are building.

The two must agree. Every major component in your design should show up somewhere on your roadmap, and every phase on your roadmap should trace back to your charter's scope and objectives.

This is not a detailed schedule and not a final design. Expect both to change. The goal is a shared, credible plan your team, your client, and your instructor can react to now — while changes are still cheap.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this assignment, you will be able to:

- Decompose a project's scope into phases, major deliverables, and milestones.
- Sequence work based on dependencies, risk, and value — not just the order things occur to you.
- Communicate a product's design at a level a client or non-technical stakeholder can review.
- Trace design decisions back to the requirements and objectives in your charter.
- Identify the biggest unknowns early and plan how and when you will resolve them.

## Examples and Tips

### Examples

Use these examples to calibrate the level of detail — not as templates to copy. Your project is different, and your materials should reflect that.

### Tips

1\. Start from the charter. Pull your scope items and objectives into a list first, then group them into phases. If something in your charter has no home on the roadmap, either add it or explicitly defer it.

2\. Plan backward from fixed dates. Course milestones and the final handoff don't move. Work backward from them.

3\. Front-load the unknowns. If you've never used a technology, can't yet access client data, or aren't sure a feature is feasible, schedule that investigation in the first phase.

4\. Leave slack. Plan for exams, breaks, illness, and client delays. A roadmap with no buffer is a roadmap that will slip.

5\. Draw before you write. Sketch the structure diagram as a team on a whiteboard before anyone builds it in a tool. Disagreements that surface here are the ones you want to find now.

6\. Write for your client. If your client couldn't understand a diagram without you in the room, simplify it or add a sentence explaining it.

7\. Make roadmap and design agree. A quick check: for each box in your structure diagram, point to where it gets built on the roadmap.
